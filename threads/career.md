## How to prepare your Curriculum Vitae?

How to prepare your Curriculum Vitae?

Your curriculum vitae (Latin for "course of life") is the most important document 📜 for all sorts of career opportunities. But not all students have access to resources for learning to write a good CV.

Check out the thread 🧵below!

*Include a photo of yourself?*

Be aware of the culture differences when it comes to including a headshot photo in your CV. It may be standard in your regions (e.g., Europe, Asia), but it's weird if you are applying positions in US and UK (for anti-discrimination reasons).

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1433651077371637771-1.mp4)

*Add hyperlinks*

It's 2021. Don't treat your CV as a plain text document. Add hyperlinks throughout your CV and make it easy for the readers to learn more about you and your work, e.g., personal website, email, project page, paper link, videos, and code on GitHub.

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1433651090755670017-1.mp4)

*Make good tables*

Tables are great for presenting structured data (e.g., your education/work history). But spend some time learning to make a one.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1433651102893977608-1.jpg)](media/1433651102893977608-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1433651102893977608-1.mp4)

*Provide context*

Include background information to help the readers (e.g., admission committee) understand your achievement. Some examples: rank X (out of how many)? GPA X (equivalence in 4.0 scale?), accuracy of 95% (on what task? and how well does baseline work?).

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1433651115623698433-1.jpg)](media/1433651115623698433-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1433651115623698433-1.mp4)

*Describe your work*

In each of your project, talk about (1) what the problem is? (2) what did you do? and (3) what the impact/results are? Most of the time (3) is missing. It's hard for people to judge the significance of your work without (3).

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1433651128311365632-1.jpg)](media/1433651128311365632-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1433651128311365632-1.mp4)

*Pick a good LaTeX template*

Pick a visually pleasing template to work with. Usually there is no page limit for your CV. Don't sacrifice readability by squeezing contents into 1-2 pages.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1433651141049569280-1.jpg)](media/1433651141049569280-1.mp4)

*Self-rate skills?*

Listing your skills is fine, but I don't really know what "4.2 stars Python" or "3.5 stars C++" on your CV mean.

*Seek feedback*

Many student-led organization offer help to review and provide feedback on your CV and other application material (particularly for graduate school applications). Check them out before you submit the application!

## How to find a research internship?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1438337355031719941> · 2021-09-16 · 共 9 条串文

How to find a research internship?

Doing an internship helps
• expand your network 🕸️,
• explore new topics 💡,
• learn new research skills 🔬, and
• earn more money 💰!

But how can we find good internship opportunities? 🤔
A thread 👇👇👇

*Find a supportive advisor*

Choose an advisor that encourages/supports/allows students doing summer internships. Some may require you to work on funded projects during the summers or delay graduation should you do internships.

Reach out to prior students and learn more!

How to intern?

Research internships are great opportunities to do cool research in industrial labs! But it's definitely not easy to complete a solid work in 3 months.

Some tips for having a successful internship ... 🧵

*Get ready*

Reach out to your mentors *before* starting your internship.
• understand what the topics will be
• get yourself familiar with the literature
• reminder your mentors to iron out a detailed internship plan

👉 Make sure that you hit the ground running!

How to find research opportunities?

Finding opportunities to gain experience is arguably the most challenging part for students wishing to pursue grad school, particularly for those who don't have resources/connections.

Some tips on approaching potential mentors. 🧵

![image](media/1627191986091438080-1.jpg)

*Lead with value*

❌ "I am very interested ... I want to grow ... I want to get the experiences... I have many prior experiences ... me, me, me, me, me, me, me!"

✅ "Here is what I can contribute to your team. I can do this bc my experience/skills on blah blah blah."

## How to prepare for a graduate school interview?

How to prepare graduate school interview?

After refreshing GradCafe pages and your email sufficient number of times, you are getting an 🎊INTERVIEW🎊 with your potential advisor at your dream university! 🤯

Congrats! But how to do well in those interviews? Some tips below 🧵

*Be prepared*

Some faculty may say "it's just chatting". THEY LIED! They will seriously evaluate the interaction with you during the call and form their opinions.

Prepare slides summarizing your work and practice commonly asked questions (e.g., research, goals, why here).

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1482238909253922817-1.jpg)](media/1482238909253922817-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1482238909253922817-1.mp4)

*Express interest*

Dept. often can only admit a limited number of applicants. So they have to strategize who to give out the offers. The level of interests you express plays an important role. You won't get an offer if the dept. feels that the chance of recruiting you is low.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1482238917168541696-1.jpg)](media/1482238917168541696-1.mp4)

The email correspondences with the faculty send out signals of how organized you are. Send concise, clear, well-formatted emails. Share your availability with specific time slots (converted to their timezone). Help them better manage their time.

The interview is bi-directional. You are also interviewing with the faculty/university. Do your homework. Ask specific questions that matter most to you.

## How do I maximize my chance for PhD programs?

So You Think You Want to Apply for a PhD

The application cycle is coming up. How do I maximize my chance of getting admitted to my dream program? 🤔

Check out the thread below for resources/advices/tips!

*Casey Fiesler @cfiesler's videos on PhD applications*

EVERYTHING you need to know about PhD applications! These videos are beyond awesome! (I wish I know these when I applied!)

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLPA3GFqdHv_oL8gRg-44TmvMzjcFRMH4I

*It's all about research*

Do NOT spend too much time maximizing your GPAs (e.g., by taking easy courses) or the Greatly Ridiculous Exam (GRE) scores. Show your potential of doing good research!

Not sure if you love doing research yet? Then why are you applying?

You won't be admitted to a program where there is no faculty who can be your advisor. Identify 1-3 potential advisors in the department (and mention them in your SOP). For CS applicants, check http://csrankings.org/

Come on, it's 2021 already. Make a personal website where you can host all of your projects, experiences, and writing samples. Include your email so that people can reach out to you.

Don't try pretending to be interested in a field just because it's "hot" or "trendy". Highlight your strength but don't oversell.

*Show, don't tell*

Don't just say "I am passionate in XXX."
->
What problems/projects have you already done? How do these experiences lead you to this place? What specific directions you are interested in pursuing in your PhD? and WHY?

Avoid Did Well It Class (D.W.I.C) letters at all cost. Give your letter writers time to work on the letters. Talk with them about your goals/interests and send them your SOP so that they can help you write the best letters.

Check their website about their thoughts on sending emails to him/her.

## How to schedule your defense?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1896673265378308497> · 2025-03-03 · 共 5 条串文

How to schedule your thesis defense?

So you think publishing top-tier papers is hard? Wait until you need to schedule your prelim/defense!

Some common mistakes and tips:

*Generation vs Verification?*

Do NOT send when2meet/doodle to everyone with so many options.

You are effectively asking busy academics to cave out 10 mins to label ALL of their available time and put all of them *on hold* for you.

Ain't nobody got time for that.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1896673272869331052-1.jpg)](media/1896673272869331052-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1896673272869331052-1.mp4)

*Use verification instead*

Instead of asking people to generate all the available time slots, ask them to verify if certain time slots work for them.

Make sure to remove hard constraints like courses/travel.

Verification is waaaay easier.

You will get responses in no time!

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1896673281543213138-1.jpg)](media/1896673281543213138-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1896673281543213138-1.mp4)

*Be specific*

Don’t ask for general time slots during a week. Academics are creatures that value freedom.

Their calendar is a hot mess! Interviews, talks, travel, committee meetings on forming another committee. Every week is different.

Ask for specific date/time instead.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1896673290623823921-1.jpg)](media/1896673290623823921-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1896673290623823921-1.mp4)

*Sent calendar invite right away*

Get a consensus? Congrats!

Send the calendar invite immediately and secure the confirmation. Academics are like goldfish and will forget about it as soon as they archive your email thread.

Anything that is not on their calendar does not exist!
