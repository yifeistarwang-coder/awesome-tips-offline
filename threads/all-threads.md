## How to do experiments?

How to do experiments?

Junior students often feel stressed before the weekly meeting with their advisors because their experiments do not go well. 😩😰😱

Some tips on why, what, and how to do experiments. 🧵

![image](media/1647098218172252160-1.jpg)

*Why? 🤔*

❌  Do an experiment to get improved performance.
✅  Do an experiment to test a hypothesis.

Many students trying to show improved results with experiments are missing the point.

Your goal of experiments should be to validate/test your research questions.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098226196004864-1.jpg)](media/1647098226196004864-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098226196004864-1.mp4)

*What? 🤔*

What experiments should we do?

This involves three main steps:
1⃣ identify key research questions
2⃣break them down into baby steps
3⃣design experiments that best answer those questions

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098233330491392-1.jpg)](media/1647098233330491392-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098233330491392-1.mp4)

*How? 🤔*

Okay, so now we know why and what, how about HOW?

There are no universal answers, but here are some principles I found helpful.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098240632758273-1.jpg)](media/1647098240632758273-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098240632758273-1.mp4)

*One thing at a time*

Remember that the goal of your experiment is to test a hypothesis?

If you change multiple things at once, then you are trying to validate a complicated hypothesis with multiple factors.

The outcomes of your experiments thus have little value.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098253056327682-1.jpg)](media/1647098253056327682-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098253056327682-1.mp4)

*Anticipate the results*

Learn to predict the outcome *before* you do the experiments.

What do you expect to see if you add X / remove Y / test on Z?

This helps you interpret the outcomes and identify errors.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098260413202435-1.jpg)](media/1647098260413202435-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098260413202435-1.mp4)

*Start with a baseline*

Very often, you do experiments to see a "change" (either positive or negative).

You will be in trouble later if you don't have a solid baseline first.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098272056475649-1.jpg)](media/1647098272056475649-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098272056475649-1.mp4)

*Document everything*

Use your brain to think, reason, and not store tedious information.

Organize your experiments with some frameworks like Hydra. https://hydra.cc/

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098283980988417-1.jpg)](media/1647098283980988417-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098283980988417-1.mp4)

相关链接：

*Two valid design choices? Do both!*

Students often ask: "Should I do A or B?"

Me: You don't need to ask me. If both A and B are sensible choices, do BOTH and let the results tell the story.

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098291849494529-1.mp4)

*Ask for feedback*

Your mentors/advisors are there to help you succeed.

Don't hesitate to seek help from them!

These tips help me tremendously when navigating complex problems. Hope they help your work as well!

Happy experimenting!

## How to get unstuck?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1717381151508054029> · 2023-10-26 · 共 11 条串文

How to get unstuck?

"It doesn't work." 🤷‍♀️🤷‍♂️

In most research projects, ~99% of the time your experiments DO NOT work. What should we do to get ourselves unstuck? 🤔

Sharing some tips I found useful. 🧵

*Help your mentors help you*

Tell them:
1⃣ WHY did you do the experiments?
2⃣ HOW did you do the experiments?
3⃣ WHAT did you see from the results?

Your mentors have more experience than you and are willing to help.

But you have to help them help you.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381158755860585-1.jpg)](media/1717381158755860585-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381158755860585-1.mp4)

*Make your experiments easy to understand*

If you only verbally describe why/how/what you did, it's difficult for others to fully understand the process.

👉 Visualize your workflow
👉 Document the details

Your mentors are more likely to catch some issues here and there.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381170399166661-1.jpg)](media/1717381170399166661-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381170399166661-1.mp4)

*Show HOW it failed*

❌ It does not work.

✅ Here is the input and our expected results. But here are our current results. If I use another more challenging input case, it fails even more.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381182260645942-1.jpg)](media/1717381182260645942-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381182260645942-1.mp4)

*Simplify the tasks until it works*

Progressively make your tasks simpler until the tasks become so trivial that they must "work".

Examples:
- multiple ➡️ single
- general ➡️ specific
- large ➡️ small
- sparse ➡️dense
- complex ➡️ simple

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381189978214801-1.jpg)](media/1717381189978214801-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381189978214801-1.mp4)

*Introduce privileged information*

Need to estimate A and B in your task?

What if you know B and only need to estimate A?

What if you know A and only need to estimate B?

Adding privileged information is a powerful trick to make progress.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381197007896752-1.jpg)](media/1717381197007896752-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381197007896752-1.mp4)

*Find the solution online*

LMGTFY

Did anyone else in the world experience similar blockers you have?

Chances are the solution is just one click away!

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381204087865825-1.jpg)](media/1717381204087865825-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381204087865825-1.mp4)

*Learn from others*

Read relevant papers solving similar tasks.

Why did their method work? What's the difference you missed?

- preprocessing steps?
- hyper-parameter settings?
- implementation tricks?

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381215785799683-1.jpg)](media/1717381215785799683-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381215785799683-1.mp4)

*Debug the idea*

I have never had a case where my first idea for a problem "just worked".

When you debug all your code/experiments and still find it not working, take a step back and debug the idea.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381222983233940-1.jpg)](media/1717381222983233940-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381222983233940-1.mp4)

*Iterate fast*

Find toy examples and proxy tasks so you can have a short turnaround time.

The faster you can iterate, the more progress you can make.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381230675534079-1.jpg)](media/1717381230675534079-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381230675534079-1.mp4)

Hope this helps you get unstuck! You can do it!

## How to decide what to work on?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1628521223838654464> · 2023-02-22 · 共 7 条串文

How to decide what to work on?

So many exciting ideas, so little time! How can one narrow down to a specific idea to work on? 🤔

Sharing some strategies I found useful. 🧵

*Find your position*

Understand your unfair advantages over others, what the community cares about, and how you can best contribute.

Avoid crowding the ball (aka chasing the hottest problem in the field).

▶ [视频（点击播放）](media/1628521225059201026-1.mp4)

*Identify intrinsic motivation*

Work on problems and real applications that you genuinely care about.

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1628521233783394304-1.mp4)

Instead of focusing on what everyone is working on NOW, anticipate the major problems LATER when disruptive technologies (e.g., new sensors, algorithms, models, data) emerge.

## How to keep track of literature?

How to keep track with the literature?

Getting started with research but overwhelmed by THOUSANDS of papers each year? How could one stay sane keeping track of the literature? 😱

Here are some tricks I found useful. 🧵

*Track the people, not the papers*

There are far fewer key people who are driving the field forward than the number of papers. Check out who the authors are when you read papers. Overtime you will recognize the important ones.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1426039204560113664-1.jpg)](media/1426039204560113664-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1426039204560113664-1.mp4)

*Read papers with good related work*

A good related work section saves you so much time by providing a clear, organized view for prior work.

*Organize the papers*

Don't read papers individually. Think about how are they related (similar in some aspects, but different in others). It often helps to build a table with columns specifying ATTRIBUTES.

With this table, reading new papers becomes easy (just add more rows).

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1426039225686896642-1.jpg)](media/1426039225686896642-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1426039225686896642-1.mp4)

*Avoid reading the paper*

Instead of spending time reading the actual paper, find resources that are much easier to digest, e.g., a talk, a youtube video, teaser results, introductory video, or an overview figure.

Very often understanding the gist of the paper is all you need.

*Read with a purpose*

Before investing time on reading a paper, think about WHY you are reading it. Are you reading for the experimental setup, the organization, the story, the style, the method, or the visualization?

You almost never need to read a paper from top to bottom.

## How to come up with research ideas?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1423499757591400448> · 2021-08-06 · 共 9 条串文

How to come up with research ideas?

Excited about starting doing research but have no clue?🤷‍♂️🤷🏻‍♀️ Here are some simple methods that I found useful in identifying initial directions.

Check out the thread below 👇

*Find a different dimension*

Just learn a cool idea from others? Think about how you could extend it to another dimension.

Ex: Text / audio / image / video / graph

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499766969901057-1.jpg)](media/1423499766969901057-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499766969901057-1.mp4)

*Relax assumptions*

Identify the underlying assumptions of existing work and try relaxing them to make it work in more unconstrained settings.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499775433920512-1.jpg)](media/1423499775433920512-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499775433920512-1.mp4)

*Make more assumptions*

Take a general approach and tailor it to your SPECIFIC problem. You can then leverage all the domain knowledge (i.e., make more assumptions) to improve the method.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499783713480706-1.jpg)](media/1423499783713480706-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499783713480706-1.mp4)

*Combine two ideas/problems*

"To steal ideas from one person is plagiarism. To steal from many is research." - Wilson Mizner

![image](media/1423499787714842627-1.jpg)

*Grab a powerful hammer and find all the nails*

Pay attention to new emerging tools in the community.  Apply and adapt them on your problem.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499796430602241-1.jpg)](media/1423499796430602241-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499796430602241-1.mp4)

*Add an adjective*

Given an existing idea X, add an adjective to make it
- slow➡️ fast
- batch➡️online
- sensitive➡️robust
- centralized➡️distributed
- single-step➡️progressive
- single-level➡️hierarchical
- fixed➡️adaptive, sth-aware
- data-hungry➡️data-efficient
and so on

*Stress test the state-of-the-art*

Don't simply run on the fixed, boring benchmark datasets. Try it out on diverse, unconstrained examples and see how it fails. It's a great way to identify limitations of existing work. This is where your work can fill the gap.

## How to cope with paper rejection?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1443248831102136333> · 2021-09-29 · 共 7 条串文

How to cope with paper rejection?

Rejection SUCKS! It feels awful that months of hard work did not pay off. 😭 How do we hold a positive outlook when dealing with rejection?

A thread of lessons (learned from many rejections) 🧵

*It’s okay to be upset*

Rejection hurts, literally. Rejection activates the same brain regions as we are physically hurt. So it's perfectly okay to feel upset. Talk with friends/lab mates/advisor. Take a day off and come back recharged.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1443248839314530307-1.jpg)](media/1443248839314530307-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1443248839314530307-1.mp4)

*It’s normal*

For junior students, a paper rejection can feel like a major setback. It's just part of the process.

"Brick walls are there for a reason. The brick walls are not there to keep us out. The brick walls are there to show how badly we want something." - Randy Pausch

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1443248847011069964-1.jpg)](media/1443248847011069964-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1443248847011069964-1.mp4)

*It’s not personal.*

Don’t take rejection personal. It's about your ideas, implementation, experiments, and writing. Create a to-do list from the reviewers' feedbacks and make sure that those concerns are properly addressed in your revision.

[![视频预览](media/1443248872877445130-1.jpg)](media/1443248872877445130-1.mp4)

▶ [视频（点击播放）](media/1443248872877445130-1.mp4)

*It doesn't matter much.*

Vast majority of the papers won't matter in the long run. Your career will be shaped only by a few good ones. Instead of getting an "okay" paper accepted, it could be a blessing in disguise to revise and strengthen your paper.

You failed because you tried! Create a CV of failures to normalize all these setbacks.

Celebrating small wins keeps up your motivation while pursuing your goals (whatever they are). Maximize the fun in your journey.

## How to Get Citations?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1997882311790968896> · 2025-12-08 · 共 11 条串文

How to get citations?

"Citations don't matter", says the one with gazillions of citations.

"We believe in slow science", say the groups who publish one paper every week.

Like it or not, the system evaluates researchers with citations.

Here are practical steps for junior folks 🧵

Three key ideas:

1⃣ Make your work accessible

2⃣ Make your work memorable

3⃣ Make your work usable

Let's break them down.

*How do you make your work more accessible?*

👉 Disseminate your work through dynamic, engaging media rather than relying solely on a static, boring PDF.

But HOW?

Make a website, write a social media thread, write a blog post, create a video explainer.

![image](media/1997882319122894865-1.png)

*How to make your work memorable?*

There are several actionable steps.

The simplest one? Come up with a good name!

## How to write the Introduction?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1638029709073166336> · 2023-03-21 · 共 20 条串文

How to write the Introduction?

As a junior student, writing the introduction of a research paper is arguably the most daunting part of paper writing. 😱

Here is a simple template I find useful:

3 Figures 🖼️ + 5 Questions 🤔

*🖼️ WHAT figure*

Start with the paper with a WHAT figure (known as the "teaser").

This figure shows only two things.
1⃣ Input
2⃣Output

This helps the readers understand what your work is about. Here are some of my favorite examples.

WHAT Example 1:

Mask R-CNN showcases its key results upfront without waiting until the result section.

1⃣ Input: Single images
2⃣Output: Instance segmentation masks

![image](media/1638029711359070208-1.jpg)

WHAT Example 2:

CycleGAN shows its ability to solve various unpaired image-to-image translation problems.

1⃣ Input: Images from domain A
2⃣Output: Images from domain B

![image](media/1638029713632313344-1.jpg)

WHAT Example 3:

Obstruction removal paper (https://alex04072000.github.io/SOLD/)
shows

1⃣ Input: Obstructed images (reflection, fence, raindrop)
2⃣Output: Clean images

Since the images are aligned, we can use split frames to 1) save space and 2) highlight the contrast.

![image](media/1638029715842768896-1.jpg)

相关链接：
- <https://alex04072000.github.io/SOLD/>

*🖼️ WHY figure*

This figure provides the "motivation" for your work.

The best WHY figure illustrates the existing work's core issue/problem with a concrete example.

Here are some of my favorite examples.

WHY Example 1:

The Shiftable Multiscale Transforms paper shows the NEED for translation invariance with a concrete example.

(a) Input signal
(b-d) Coefficients of wavelet representations.

(e) Shifted the input signal by ONE sample
(f-h) LOOK! Completely DIFFERENT coefficients

![image](media/1638029718724247555-1.jpg)

WHY Example 2

The Relative Attributes work highlights the NEED for a more informative and intuitive description using relative attributes with concrete examples.

![image](media/1638029720523616258-1.jpg)

WHY Example 3

The Feature Pyramid Networks work shows multiple alternative design options (a-c) and their drawbacks. This helps motivate the NEED for their design of a fast & accurate model.

![image](media/1638029722067116034-1.jpg)

*🖼️ HOW figure*

This figure describes how your method works.

Some tips:
✅ Link all sections
✅ Use consistent notations
✅ Self-contained caption
✅ Visualize the variables
✅ Cascade of small units: "Input -> some processing -> Output" (think about computational graph).

HOW Example 1:

The HyperReel paper (https://hyperreel.github.io/) visualizes the four main algorithmic steps.

Note the math notations, name, and detailed, self-contained figure caption.

![image](media/1638029724793307139-1.jpg)

相关链接：
- <https://hyperreel.github.io/>

HOW Example 2:

My video completion work back in my Ph.D. years https://filebox.ece.vt.edu/~jbhuang/project/vidcomp/
shows how the processing steps in each iteration.

![image](media/1638029727091892226-1.jpg)

相关链接：
- <https://filebox.ece.vt.edu/~jbhuang/project/vidcomp/>

HOW Example 3:

The Robust Dynamic Radiance Fields paper (https://robust-dynrf.github.io/) shows how all the math notations and variables connect with each other using this HOW figure. Note the descriptive figure caption.

![image](media/1638029729889501184-1.jpg)

相关链接：
- <https://robust-dynrf.github.io/>

With the WHAT-WHY-HOW figures, we are now ready to answer the following five questions!

🤔 What's the problem?
🤔 What have others done?
🤔 What's the gap?
🤔 What have you done?
🤔 What do you contribute?

*🤔 Q1: What's the problem?*

✅ State clearly what problem you are addressing.

✅ Tell the readers explicitly why they should care about the problem (e.g., applications).

🖼️ Use your WHAT figure for visual references.

*🤔 Q2: What have others done?*

✅ Describe what other solutions (SOTAs) are to your problem.

✅ Usually follows a historical trajectory, e.g., classical geometric methods did X, learning-based methods did Y, and recent hybrid methods did Z.

*🤔 Q3: What's the gap?*

✅ Explain why all these existing solutions are NOT satisfactory (in some aspects). This is important as your work often addresses a specific gap/flaw/drawback of existing work.

🖼️ Use the example in your WHY figure to illustrate the *gap*.

*🤔 Q4: What have you done?*

✅ Add "In this paper, ..." at the beginning of this paragraph. It helps provide a clean separation of the past/current work.

✅ Talk about a) Task, b) High-level idea, c) Evaluation

🖼️ Use your HOW figure to ground your discussions.

*🤔 Q5: What do you contribute?*

✅ Not everything you do is "novel" e.g., a large part of your work may build upon some existing methods. Thus, it is a good practice to explicitly state your contributions.

✅ Make a list so that lazy reviewers can use them in their reviews 😜

In sum, the introduction section would look like this:

🤔 What's the problem? (see 🖼️ WHAT figure)

🤔 What have others done?

🤔 What's the gap? (see 🖼️ WHY figure)

🤔 What have you done? (see 🖼️ HOW figure)

🤔 What do you contribute?

Hope this helps!

*Active voice*

Friends don’t let friends use passive voice!

Using passive voice hides the subject and creates ambiguous, indirect, and wordy sentences. Things don't "get done" by themselves. Take responsibility for what you do and use active voice whenever possible.

We often use "however" as "nevertheless/in spite of that" in a paper. Do NOT start with a sentence with however to avoid confusion. Why?

When starting with however, it means "in whatever manner/way" or "to whatever degree/extent".

How to draw an overview figure?

Creating a clear and informative overview figure is crucial for visualizing HOW your method works.

But how? 🤔 Let's deep dive with 🐢

![image](media/1665738070002483201-1.jpg)

*Choose the right level of abstraction*

Simplifying complex procedures helps improve clarity.

Ask yourself what the key message you want to convey. Don't overwhelm your readers with unnecessary details.

![image](media/1665738072196096003-1.jpg)

*Think in terms of computational graph*

Most methods process some INPUT with some COMPUTATION to produce some OUTPUT.

Visualize the flow with a "computational graph".

• Nodes: Computation
• Arrows: Dependency

![image](media/1665738074964258817-1.jpg)

*Use an example*

While your overview figure illustrates an *abstract* workflow, visualizing the intermediate variables/data with a *concrete* example makes it easier to understand.

![image](media/1665738077552140288-1.jpg)

*Follow the left-to-right direction*

Most languages read from left to right. ⏩⏩

Following this direction makes your figure "read" better.

![image](media/1665738080052027393-1.jpg)

*Apply formatting strategically*

Be purposeful when you use formatting to highlight similarity, grouping, and contrast.

Make sure that you don't overdo it.

![image](media/1665738082321039364-1.jpg)

*Use proper font size*

Use consistent and clearly visible font size.

Don't make your overview figure a vision test. 🧐

![image](media/1665738085202640897-1.jpg)

*Use consistent formatting*

• Capitalize the first character for the first word?
• Capitalize the first character for every word?
• All caps?
• All lowercase?

Pick one and stick with it.

![image](media/1665738087744299008-1.jpg)

*Align everything*

Tiny bits of misalignment here and there distract your readers. (Look how annoying this looks like!)

Align the positions, spacing, and sizes.

![image](media/1665738090395193346-1.jpg)

*Use consistent styles*

Put the label
• below the image?
• above the image?
• within the image?
• to the right of the image?

Pick one and be consistent.

![image](media/1665738093121421314-1.jpg)

*Provide a roadmap for the paper*

Add labels for sections, equations, and figures to provide a roadmap for the paper.

![image](media/1665738095965229061-1.jpg)

*Be explicit about the dependency/computation*

When you merge two arrows, it's unclear what happened there.

The example above obscures this process (it looks like the module takes two inputs simultaneously).

## How to create a good table?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1626372600824844289> · 2023-02-17 · 共 8 条串文

How to create a good table?

While in grad school, I thought my job writing the paper was done after dumping all the numerical numbers from my experiments in a table. 🤦‍♂️

Check out some tips that will help you improve the quality of your tables! 🧵

*1⃣ Avoid vertical lines*

Having vertical lines in a table almost always makes the table less readable. Avoid them at all costs.

![image](media/1626372602259206144-1.jpg)

*2⃣ Never, ever use double rules*

Feel the urge to use double rules (\hline)?

Try the \toprule, \midrule, \bottomrule using the booktabs package instead! It levels up your table in no time!

![image](media/1626372604440326145-1.jpg)

*3⃣ Label the columns*

Add up/down arrows to let readers know how to interpret the numbers.

Add units to let your readers understand what the numbers mean.

![image](media/1626372606596206593-1.jpg)

*4⃣ Align everything*

• Align texts to the left
• Align numbers to the center
• Align numbers at consistent decimal point

![image](media/1626372608747864065-1.jpg)

*5⃣ Grouping results*

Have two or more groups of results and feel the urge again to separate them using vertical lines?

\multicolumn comes to the rescue!

Remember to use \cmidrule to group the right columns!

![image](media/1626372611163684867-1.jpg)

*6⃣ Encode rows with attributes*

One table, one message. Don't mix all the results together.

If possible, encode different rows using *attributes* so that it's easy for readers to understand/compare results from different rows.

![image](media/1626372613441290240-1.jpg)

Hope this helps!

Having a clean and clear table definitely gets your message across more effectively!

How to cite papers?

Citing papers properly
👉 gives credit where credit's due,
👉 provides supporting evidence of your claim, and
👉 presents an organized view of related work.

Sharing some tips I found useful. 🧵

"Parenthetical": removing the citations, your sentences should still make sense.

When writing the related work, focus on the STORY and cite relevant papers along the way.

This provides an organized structure of how individual papers are connected.

This style often starts with authors' name in your sentence.

Con: Easily leads to a poorly written "laundry list".

How to present a line plot?

Line plots are effective for describing the relationship between two variables of interests.

Unfortunately, most junior students would simply copy&paste the figure from the paper in their talk and cause much confusion. 😕

❌ Show the entire plot. Waaaay too much to unpack.

✅ Show *only the X-Y axis* (no lines yet!). Describe what factors you SUSPECT have an impact on the Y-axis, e.g., time, model complexity, dataset size.

Sharing tips on preparing your presentation slides

Just attend many thesis presentations and qual exams at the end of the semester. I compiled some common pitfalls here and hopefully would be helpful to some.

Check out the thread 🧵below!

*Outline*

I am surprised to see so many talks starting with the OUTLINE.

No one, literally no one, will be excited by the: "I will first introduce the problem, then I discuss related work, next I present our method, I show some results, and conclude the talk".

![image](media/1397058831113457666-1.jpg)

*Be concise*

Do not treat your slides as a script.

Rule of thumbs for my students preparing a talk:
• Never write full sentences (unless quoting)
• Always write one-liners
• No more three lines of texts per slides

The tables in your paper/thesis are very informative with all the citations and compared methods. This is great. But, it's a disaster to present them as is in your talk.

No one knows what [17], [39] mean. Highlight and interpret the key results for your audience.

![image](media/1397058841938956288-1.jpg)

*Figures*

Explain how to read your plots. What does x-axis/y-axis mean? What do different lines mean? What can we learn from this plot?

If you plan to skip the discussion of some figures, just remove them.

*Informative slides title*

Don't use the most salient part of slides to show "Results", "Visual comparison", "Ablation study"

The title should describe the TAKEAWAY message from that slide.

## How to design your presentation?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1865199644415832424> · 2024-12-07 · 共 6 条串文

How to design your presentation?

Presentation is an essential skill for academics.

After attending so many meetings, prelims, thesis defenses, research talks, and lectures, I’ve realized that I may have been approaching it all wrong ...

Some (bitter) lessons I learned. 👇

*Slide title?*

"Every slide should have an informative title."

I used to think that way, too, but recently, I’ve realized that it greatly limits flexibility.

Instead, think of slides as visual aids. Removing all titles from my slides dramatically improved their clarity.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1865199651650933062-1.jpg)](media/1865199651650933062-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1865199651650933062-1.mp4)

*Bullet points?*

"Summarize your talking points as a list."

That "click to add text" prompt in an empty slide can tempt you to turn everything into a list of bullet points.

But it dilutes your message and makes it harder to follow. Instead, focus on presenting one idea at a time.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1865199658886152554-1.jpg)](media/1865199658886152554-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1865199658886152554-1.mp4)

*Transitions*

I used to make slides, so each slide had its content/topic/title. But this meant that every time I advanced a slide, the audience's attention was RESET, disrupting their focus.

Now, I fully embrace morph transitions, creating a natural, continuous flow of ideas.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1865199665982951512-1.jpg)](media/1865199665982951512-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1865199665982951512-1.mp4)

*Tool-oriented vs. problem-oriented*

When teaching a topic, I often feel compelled to present tech background A, B, C, only to conclude why they matter to problem X later.

But that’s so backward—Why should people care?

Instead, tell a story on how to solve a problem and discuss all the tools/algorithms/backgrounds along the way.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1865199673251684678-1.jpg)](media/1865199673251684678-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1865199673251684678-1.mp4)

*And then vs. But, therefore*

In a talk, you might have topics A, B, and C. I used to present them in order—A, then B, and then C.

But, that’s horrible for keeping attention. 😱

Instead, tell a story about topic A. But sth terribly wrong happened, therefore we need topic B. But, B also has this serious issue. Therefore, it leads us to topic C.

## How to revise an academic poster?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1974198882906325054> · 2025-10-03 · 共 18 条串文

How to Revise an Academic Poster?

Designing a clear poster is an essential skill for students. We recently revised one for a paper presented last week.

BUT, instead of just one student benefiting from the process, I’m sharing it and hope some will find it helpful. 🧵

In the initial draft, it's common to simply include most of the figures and tables from the paper on the poster.

This is a good start.

BUT, this often results in too much detail, making the poster less effective at communicating the main message.

![image](media/1974198884621824044-1.jpg)

In the 2nd draft, we highlight the key messages (problem, key idea, results).

BUT, there are many design/layout/writing choices that we can improve.

Let's take a closer look at them! Shall we?

![image](media/1974198888035946975-1.jpg)

Many posters have these types of school logos.

BUT, nobody really knows them, and they are simply too small to be seen. 🔍

A better way is to simply spell out the affiliations instead.

![image](media/1974198891412328647-1.png)

The author list is important.

As a junior student, you want to make this part clear and obvious so that other people remember you.

Details:
- make it large
- replace commas with spaces
- fix the superscript
- use line breaks so that the author list is center-aligned.

![image](media/1974198894017065439-1.jpg)

Here, @JayLEE_0301 started with a question.

BUT, there are two problems with this question.
- 1) unclear what "structured" or "human-like" means
- 2) the sentence has TWO lines

Always write one-liners! Ain't nobody got time to read!

![image](media/1974198896546169204-1.jpg)

Bullet points

Whenever we use bullet points, we should use parallel writing (or parallel structure).

This helps reduce the cognitive load on your readers when parsing the text.

(not a fan of many colors, but it's okay as they help establish correspondence with other parts)

![image](media/1974198899159281891-1.jpg)

Building up correspondence!

BEFORE:
- unclear relation between title and figure
- changing icons

AFTER:
- tie the three figures with the title
- enlarge the text annotation.

![image](media/1974198901680058640-1.jpg)

Let's improve the illustration slightly.

Here, we aim to convey the concept of possible outcomes (e.g., an unsupported block will slide down).

BUT, we only show the final state.

Adding "history" and "trajectory" makes the illusion clearer.

![image](media/1974198904326660196-1.png)

Write concisely and avoid multi-line sentences at all costs!

![image](media/1974198906599977364-1.jpg)

Now to the middle column.

Here, the sentence has parallel structure. That's great!

BUT, the layout does not reinforce the parallel concept.

Let's align them so we can clearly see the parallel structure.

![image](media/1974216610417176844-1.png)

The method overview figure is tricky.

It needs to have a proper level of abstraction, yet a sufficient level of detail to illustrate how things work.

Here, we adjust the layout and add a few annotations to improve its clarity.

![image](media/1974221204052488632-1.jpg)

Unless it's a quote,  avoid writing complete sentences.

Those sentences you intended to write are something you will SAY in a presentation.

Let people pay attention to you, not just the poster/slides.

![image](media/1974222188451381694-1.jpg)

For results, the initial draft is yet another list.

I think it would be fun to present it visually for a change.

Unfortunately, I was told that robotics people are quite conservative and they will not like this.

So my revision suggestion was rejected...

![image](media/1974226235921228141-1.jpg)

BEFORE

Two issues with the original result presentation
- unclear correspondence because the texts are not on the same line
- the blue color is a bit arbitrary

AFTER
- fix the alignment
- user green to invoke the concept of *gain* (from stock markets)

![image](media/1974228128579891348-1.jpg)

When presenting results in tables, be sure to include annotations (e.g., success rate) to provide context.

Without such information, it can be difficult for readers to interpret the results accurately.

![image](media/1974228980338237610-1.jpg)

Let's put all these together. Here is the revised poster.

There are still many areas for improvement, such as the overview figure, excessive text, excessive colors, and layout design.

But, with these changes, it's now a decent poster presentation!

![image](media/1974233905315119219-1.jpg)

How to communicate clearly?

Majority of our time goes into emails, slack messages, and loads of meetings.

Why is it relevant or important for your work/project/team? Why should your audience care? 🤷‍♂️🤷‍♀️

👉 Tell me why?

*(3) Now What?*

What are the *actionable* steps next?
e.g., Set up another meeting? Ask for feedback? Outline a plan for a new task?

👉 Call for specific action!

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1520615220271538176-1.jpg)](media/1520615220271538176-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1520615220271538176-1.mp4)

Example: Research

1⃣ What?
Here are the results of a toy problem A.

2⃣ So what?
This is important because the experiment helps us  study only problem A and not get distracted by problem B/C.

3⃣ Now what?
I will move on to study problem B and C.

Example: Opportunity

1⃣ What?
I am interested in working with you.

2⃣ So what?
I have relevant skills, experiences, and specific ideas on how to extend your recent work X to Y and Z.

3⃣ Now what?
When would you be available so I can present these specific directions?

Happy communicating!

Having the What/So What/Now What template in mind when writing and speaking about ANYTHING has been very helpful for keeping my messages clear and concise!

Hope this helps yours too! 👍

How do I get professors to answer my emails?

You have sent SO MANY emails to professors for scheduling your prelim, looking for research opportunities, or asking questions about courses etc. But they just don't reply at all! 😠

How do I get things done? A few tips below 🧵

*Group scheduling*

Don't send out Doodle/When2meet with 30+ entries! Look up their (course) schedules and propose only a few (3-5) time slots.

If they need to spend 15 mins filling out your scheduling request, they will simply ignore your email.

Do: I am available ... Will you be available in one of the time slots?

When you ask for availability, you are effectively asking for commitment for ALL of the time slots.

How to save for retirement as a graduate student (in the US)?

*Why?*

"Don't make fun of graduate students. They just made a terrible life choice." - The Simpsons

Yes, grad students are paid poorly. But with income from RA/TA/fellowships/internships, there usually are leftover money.

❌ Do nothing
✅ Invest in *tax-advantaged* accounts

[![视频预览](media/1510479698731716614-1.jpg)](media/1510479698731716614-1.mp4)

▶ [视频（点击播放）](media/1510479698731716614-1.mp4)

*Why save early?*

When you save and invest for retirement early, you get to enjoy the power of compounding for your investments.

Ideally, max out the contribution limit (6K a year).

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1510479705853640706-1.jpg)](media/1510479705853640706-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1510479705853640706-1.mp4)

*How to save?*

Two types of Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs):

👉 Traditional: Save now, tax later
👉 Roth: Tax now, save later

As grad students are in the low-tax bracket, Roth IRA would probably make more sense.

Earnings growth + withdrawals in retirement: *tax free*

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1510479713243963395-1.jpg)](media/1510479713243963395-1.mp4)

*Why now?*

Previously, graduate students who are on fellowship stipends or grants would have difficulty in funding their IRAs because those income are not considered "taxable compensation".

But that change in Dec 2019, making it easy for all grad students to save!

*What if I really need the money after graduation?*

✅ Withdraw Roth IRA contributions (not earnings) are tax free and penalty free.

✅ Several early withdrawals of earnings can still be tax/penalty free, e.g., 1st home purchase, large medical expenses, education.
