## How to do experiments?

How to do experiments?

Junior students often feel stressed before the weekly meeting with their advisors because their experiments do not go well. 😩😰😱

Some tips on why, what, and how to do experiments. 🧵

![image](media/1647098218172252160-1.jpg)

*Why? 🤔*

❌  Do an experiment to get improved performance.
✅  Do an experiment to test a hypothesis.

Many students trying to show improved results with experiments are missing the point.

Your goal of experiments should be to validate/test your research questions.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098226196004864-1.jpg)](media/1647098226196004864-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098226196004864-1.mp4)

*What? 🤔*

What experiments should we do?

This involves three main steps:
1⃣ identify key research questions
2⃣break them down into baby steps
3⃣design experiments that best answer those questions

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098233330491392-1.jpg)](media/1647098233330491392-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098233330491392-1.mp4)

*How? 🤔*

Okay, so now we know why and what, how about HOW?

There are no universal answers, but here are some principles I found helpful.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098240632758273-1.jpg)](media/1647098240632758273-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098240632758273-1.mp4)

*One thing at a time*

Remember that the goal of your experiment is to test a hypothesis?

If you change multiple things at once, then you are trying to validate a complicated hypothesis with multiple factors.

The outcomes of your experiments thus have little value.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098253056327682-1.jpg)](media/1647098253056327682-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098253056327682-1.mp4)

*Anticipate the results*

Learn to predict the outcome *before* you do the experiments.

What do you expect to see if you add X / remove Y / test on Z?

This helps you interpret the outcomes and identify errors.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098260413202435-1.jpg)](media/1647098260413202435-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098260413202435-1.mp4)

*Start with a baseline*

Very often, you do experiments to see a "change" (either positive or negative).

You will be in trouble later if you don't have a solid baseline first.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098272056475649-1.jpg)](media/1647098272056475649-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098272056475649-1.mp4)

*Document everything*

Use your brain to think, reason, and not store tedious information.

Organize your experiments with some frameworks like Hydra. https://hydra.cc/

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098283980988417-1.jpg)](media/1647098283980988417-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098283980988417-1.mp4)

相关链接：
- <https://hydra.cc/>

*Two valid design choices? Do both!*

Students often ask: "Should I do A or B?"

Me: You don't need to ask me. If both A and B are sensible choices, do BOTH and let the results tell the story.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098291849494529-1.jpg)](media/1647098291849494529-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098291849494529-1.mp4)

*Ask for feedback*

Your mentors/advisors are there to help you succeed.

Don't hesitate to seek help from them!

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1647098298988101633-1.jpg)](media/1647098298988101633-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1647098298988101633-1.mp4)

These tips help me tremendously when navigating complex problems. Hope they help your work as well!

If you find this thread helpful, please like, share, and follow! https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1647098218172252160

Happy experimenting!

## How to get unstuck?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1717381151508054029> · 2023-10-26 · 共 11 条串文

How to get unstuck?

"It doesn't work." 🤷‍♀️🤷‍♂️

In most research projects, ~99% of the time your experiments DO NOT work. What should we do to get ourselves unstuck? 🤔

Sharing some tips I found useful. 🧵

*Help your mentors help you*

Tell them:
1⃣ WHY did you do the experiments?
2⃣ HOW did you do the experiments?
3⃣ WHAT did you see from the results?

Your mentors have more experience than you and are willing to help.

But you have to help them help you.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381158755860585-1.jpg)](media/1717381158755860585-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381158755860585-1.mp4)

*Make your experiments easy to understand*

If you only verbally describe why/how/what you did, it's difficult for others to fully understand the process.

👉 Visualize your workflow
👉 Document the details

Your mentors are more likely to catch some issues here and there.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381170399166661-1.jpg)](media/1717381170399166661-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381170399166661-1.mp4)

*Show HOW it failed*

❌ It does not work.

✅ Here is the input and our expected results. But here are our current results. If I use another more challenging input case, it fails even more.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381182260645942-1.jpg)](media/1717381182260645942-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381182260645942-1.mp4)

*Simplify the tasks until it works*

Progressively make your tasks simpler until the tasks become so trivial that they must "work".

Examples:
- multiple ➡️ single
- general ➡️ specific
- large ➡️ small
- sparse ➡️dense
- complex ➡️ simple

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381189978214801-1.jpg)](media/1717381189978214801-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381189978214801-1.mp4)

*Introduce privileged information*

Need to estimate A and B in your task?

What if you know B and only need to estimate A?

What if you know A and only need to estimate B?

Adding privileged information is a powerful trick to make progress.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381197007896752-1.jpg)](media/1717381197007896752-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381197007896752-1.mp4)

*Find the solution online*

LMGTFY

Did anyone else in the world experience similar blockers you have?

Chances are the solution is just one click away!

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381204087865825-1.jpg)](media/1717381204087865825-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381204087865825-1.mp4)

*Learn from others*

Read relevant papers solving similar tasks.

Why did their method work? What's the difference you missed?

- preprocessing steps?
- hyper-parameter settings?
- implementation tricks?

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381215785799683-1.jpg)](media/1717381215785799683-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381215785799683-1.mp4)

*Debug the idea*

I have never had a case where my first idea for a problem "just worked".

When you debug all your code/experiments and still find it not working, take a step back and debug the idea.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381222983233940-1.jpg)](media/1717381222983233940-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381222983233940-1.mp4)

*Iterate fast*

Find toy examples and proxy tasks so you can have a short turnaround time.

The faster you can iterate, the more progress you can make.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1717381230675534079-1.jpg)](media/1717381230675534079-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1717381230675534079-1.mp4)

Hope this helps you get unstuck! You can do it!

## How to decide what to work on?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1628521223838654464> · 2023-02-22 · 共 7 条串文

How to decide what to work on?

So many exciting ideas, so little time! How can one narrow down to a specific idea to work on? 🤔

Sharing some strategies I found useful. 🧵

*Find your position*

Understand your unfair advantages over others, what the community cares about, and how you can best contribute.

Avoid crowding the ball (aka chasing the hottest problem in the field).

[![视频预览](media/1628521225059201026-1.jpg)](media/1628521225059201026-1.mp4)

▶ [视频（点击播放）](media/1628521225059201026-1.mp4)

*Identify intrinsic motivation*

Work on problems and real applications that you genuinely care about.

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1628521233783394304-1.mp4)

*Anticipate disruptive technologies*

Instead of focusing on what everyone is working on NOW, anticipate the major problems LATER when disruptive technologies (e.g., new sensors, algorithms, models, data) emerge.

At the begging of your project, be an amateur and dream big! Don't be intimidated by all the technical challenges just yet.

Not all the ideas will pan out. Design small experiments to validate your hypothesis and assumptions.

## How to keep track of literature?

How to keep track with the literature?

Getting started with research but overwhelmed by THOUSANDS of papers each year? How could one stay sane keeping track of the literature? 😱

Here are some tricks I found useful. 🧵

*Track the people, not the papers*

There are far fewer key people who are driving the field forward than the number of papers. Check out who the authors are when you read papers. Overtime you will recognize the important ones.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1426039204560113664-1.jpg)](media/1426039204560113664-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1426039204560113664-1.mp4)

*Read papers with good related work*

A good related work section saves you so much time by providing a clear, organized view for prior work.

*Organize the papers*

Don't read papers individually. Think about how are they related (similar in some aspects, but different in others). It often helps to build a table with columns specifying ATTRIBUTES.

With this table, reading new papers becomes easy (just add more rows).

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1426039225686896642-1.jpg)](media/1426039225686896642-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1426039225686896642-1.mp4)

*Avoid reading the paper*

Instead of spending time reading the actual paper, find resources that are much easier to digest, e.g., a talk, a youtube video, teaser results, introductory video, or an overview figure.

Very often understanding the gist of the paper is all you need.

*Read with a purpose*

Before investing time on reading a paper, think about WHY you are reading it. Are you reading for the experimental setup, the organization, the story, the style, the method, or the visualization?

You almost never need to read a paper from top to bottom.

## How to come up with research ideas?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1423499757591400448> · 2021-08-06 · 共 9 条串文

How to come up with research ideas?

Excited about starting doing research but have no clue?🤷‍♂️🤷🏻‍♀️ Here are some simple methods that I found useful in identifying initial directions.

Check out the thread below 👇

*Find a different dimension*

Just learn a cool idea from others? Think about how you could extend it to another dimension.

Ex: Text / audio / image / video / graph

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499766969901057-1.jpg)](media/1423499766969901057-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499766969901057-1.mp4)

*Relax assumptions*

Identify the underlying assumptions of existing work and try relaxing them to make it work in more unconstrained settings.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499775433920512-1.jpg)](media/1423499775433920512-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499775433920512-1.mp4)

*Make more assumptions*

Take a general approach and tailor it to your SPECIFIC problem. You can then leverage all the domain knowledge (i.e., make more assumptions) to improve the method.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499783713480706-1.jpg)](media/1423499783713480706-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499783713480706-1.mp4)

*Combine two ideas/problems*

"To steal ideas from one person is plagiarism. To steal from many is research." - Wilson Mizner

![image](media/1423499787714842627-1.jpg)

*Grab a powerful hammer and find all the nails*

Pay attention to new emerging tools in the community.  Apply and adapt them on your problem.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499796430602241-1.jpg)](media/1423499796430602241-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499796430602241-1.mp4)

*Add an adjective*

Given an existing idea X, add an adjective to make it
- slow➡️ fast
- batch➡️online
- sensitive➡️robust
- centralized➡️distributed
- single-step➡️progressive
- single-level➡️hierarchical
- fixed➡️adaptive, sth-aware
- data-hungry➡️data-efficient
and so on

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1423499804433391619-1.jpg)](media/1423499804433391619-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1423499804433391619-1.mp4)

*Stress test the state-of-the-art*

Don't simply run on the fixed, boring benchmark datasets. Try it out on diverse, unconstrained examples and see how it fails. It's a great way to identify limitations of existing work. This is where your work can fill the gap.

## How to cope with paper rejection?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1443248831102136333> · 2021-09-29 · 共 7 条串文

How to cope with paper rejection?

Rejection SUCKS! It feels awful that months of hard work did not pay off. 😭 How do we hold a positive outlook when dealing with rejection?

A thread of lessons (learned from many rejections) 🧵

*It’s okay to be upset*

Rejection hurts, literally. Rejection activates the same brain regions as we are physically hurt. So it's perfectly okay to feel upset. Talk with friends/lab mates/advisor. Take a day off and come back recharged.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1443248839314530307-1.jpg)](media/1443248839314530307-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1443248839314530307-1.mp4)

*It’s normal*

For junior students, a paper rejection can feel like a major setback. It's just part of the process.

"Brick walls are there for a reason. The brick walls are not there to keep us out. The brick walls are there to show how badly we want something." - Randy Pausch

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1443248847011069964-1.jpg)](media/1443248847011069964-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1443248847011069964-1.mp4)

*It’s not personal.*

Don’t take rejection personal. It's about your ideas, implementation, experiments, and writing. Create a to-do list from the reviewers' feedbacks and make sure that those concerns are properly addressed in your revision.

[![视频预览](media/1443248872877445130-1.jpg)](media/1443248872877445130-1.mp4)

▶ [视频（点击播放）](media/1443248872877445130-1.mp4)

*It doesn't matter much.*

Vast majority of the papers won't matter in the long run. Your career will be shaped only by a few good ones. Instead of getting an "okay" paper accepted, it could be a blessing in disguise to revise and strengthen your paper.

You failed because you tried! Create a CV of failures to normalize all these setbacks.

Celebrating small wins keeps up your motivation while pursuing your goals (whatever they are). Maximize the fun in your journey.

## How to Get Citations?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1997882311790968896> · 2025-12-08 · 共 11 条串文

How to get citations?

"Citations don't matter", says the one with gazillions of citations.

"We believe in slow science", say the groups who publish one paper every week.

Like it or not, the system evaluates researchers with citations.

Here are practical steps for junior folks 🧵

Three key ideas:

1⃣ Make your work accessible

2⃣ Make your work memorable

3⃣ Make your work usable

Let's break them down.

*How do you make your work more accessible?*

👉 Disseminate your work through dynamic, engaging media rather than relying solely on a static, boring PDF.

But HOW?

Make a website, write a social media thread, write a blog post, create a video explainer.

![image](media/1997882319122894865-1.png)

*How to make your work memorable?*

There are several actionable steps.

The simplest one? Come up with a good name!

*How to make your work usable?*

Your work is cited because it empowers others to do their work.

Don't simply dump your messy codebase online.
Put effort into polishing and organizing it so people can easily build upon your work.

To further increase your research impact, check out this post that I found particularly insightful:

"Build what you need and use what you build." - @Michael_J_Black
 https://x.com/Michael_J_Black/status/1782284444537090114

Share the many facets of your work.

To finish up your work, you typically need to
- write up evaluation script
- implement custom kernel
- reproduce baselines
- process messy data, clean up annotations
- visualize results

All these have values. Share generously to help others.

Of course, aiming to maximize citation counts is the WRONG goal.

Focus instead on producing research that has real impact. Over time, your work will naturally be appreciated and recognized.

Make them accessible, memorable, and useful!
