How to drive your research forward?

“I tested the idea we discussed last time. Here are some results. It does not work. (… awkward silence)”

Such conversations happen so many times when meetings with students. How do we move forward?

You need …

a *gradient vector*, a direction and magnitude that tell you where you should explore in the vast space of possible solutions.

Reporting results of your idea only tell us … nothing. It’s like evaluating the loss function with one set of parameters. 

We have no way to optimize/improve it.

[![two south park characters are standing in front of a sign that says baggage claim](media/bsky/post02-1.jpg)](media/bsky/post02-1.mp4)

First, always show what happen when you try the simplest possible baseline. 

This gives you a reference of how things are and contextualize your results.

The difference between the two gives you the *direction*.

[![Its Simple Randy Marsh GIF](media/bsky/post03-1.jpg)](media/bsky/post03-1.mp4)

Second, pretend you have access to some ground truth privileged information (eg via toy examples and synthetic experiments). This gives you some upper bound performance of your approach. 

Comparing the three sets of results gives you a sense of *magnitude*.

[![a cartoon character from south park is smiling and says find out how to use it](media/bsky/post04-1.jpg)](media/bsky/post04-1.mp4)

Thinking about approach a research problem as an optimization problem (finding and moving along gradient directions) has helped me get unstuck in many cases.

Hope this help you as well.
