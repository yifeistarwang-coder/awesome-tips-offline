## How to make a research poster?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1438948793718804486> · 2021-09-17 · 共 9 条串文

How to make a research poster?

Poster presentation is a great (and fun!) way to showcase your research, interact with researchers, and get feedback from them. But how we make a good poster? 🤔

My two cents below 🧵

*Abstract*

Poster is a VISUAL abstract. Please DO NOT dump your paper abstract to your poster. Your job is to get people excited so that they will read your paper afterward.

Your poster should have
• zero complete sentences
• one-liners only

*Links to paper/website/data*

Provide simple ways that help your audience to learn more about your work!

Examples:
• Add a QR code at the top of your poster.
• Add a *custom* short URL (no one remembers random sequence like https://bit.ly/3tPGM3x)

*Distill the key messages from your paper*

Accept the fact that you won't be able to present everything in your paper. Ruthlessly remove all the unimportant details/results.

*Align everything*

Your poster usually have multiple blocks of contents, e.g., problem, key idea, method, results. Horizontally and vertically align all the blocks so that the poster is balance and visually pleasing.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1438948827969564676-1.jpg)](media/1438948827969564676-1.mp4)

*References*

Do not simply copy-paste the large tables of results from your paper. Nobody knows what [14] [42] [57] are.

Simplify, visualize the key results. Replace numbered reference citations with [Name-Venue-Year].

*Color palette*

It does not have to be beautiful, but please don't burn others' retina...

![image](media/1438948843626893314-1.jpg)

*Important information on the top*

This is relevant only for physical conferences. When you have crowd in front your poster, only the top areas are visible for people. Value those areas more.

*Scripting*

Write down your 1-2 mins elevator pitch so you can present the gist of work quickly. It's super annoying to listen to a long and unorganized poster presentation (and feel awkward to interrupt you so they can leave).

## How to present a poster at a conference?

How to present a poster at a conference?

Poster presentation is a great way to disseminate your research findings, have in-depth discussions, and expand your networks!

Here are some tips for presenting like a pro! 👇

*Be Welcoming*

✅ Stand by the side of the poster.
✅ Never face the poster and turn your back on your audience.
✅ Actively approach people walking by.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097864601358336-1.jpg)](media/1518097864601358336-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1518097864601358336-1.mp4)

*Be Enthusiastic*

If you do not sound excited about your work, why should others be excited?

Hype yourself up! 🤗

People can feel the positive energy and will be willing to learn more about your work.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097872050339842-1.jpg)](media/1518097872050339842-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1518097872050339842-1.mp4)

*Be Early and Stay Late*

There are fewer people *before* and *after* the scheduled poster session.

Don't miss the great opportunity to have interesting, long conversation with people genuinely interested in your work.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097879574921216-1.jpg)](media/1518097879574921216-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1518097879574921216-1.mp4)

*Be Concise*

❌ Present linearly from top to bottom for 10 mins.
✅ Present an elevator pitch for 30 seconds. Hook the audience and then respond to any questions.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097887086919680-1.jpg)](media/1518097887086919680-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1518097887086919680-1.mp4)

*Use Tools*

Poster is just a *static* visual abstract.

If your work involve other *dynamic* mediums (e.g., video, sound), bringing a laptop or tablet would make your presentation a lot more engaging.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097894611595265-1.jpg)](media/1518097894611595265-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1518097894611595265-1.mp4)

*No "Do you know X?"*

Yes, your intention is good, but this only makes other people feel dumb.

Give a 10 sec background about X and then focus on *your work*.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097902022889472-1.jpg)](media/1518097902022889472-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1518097902022889472-1.mp4)

*Handout*

Print several mini-poster as handout so that you can give people who are interested in learning more.

Make sure that the poster has link/barcode that refer them to your project page.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097909488705537-1.jpg)](media/1518097909488705537-1.mp4)

▶ [GIF 动画（点击播放）](media/1518097909488705537-1.mp4)

*Build Your Network*

• Thank *each* person for stopping by your poster and/or asking good questions.
• Say their name (or ask how to pronounce their name).

👉 Now you can greet them next time and grow your connection.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097916824539137-1.jpg)](media/1518097916824539137-1.mp4)

*Bring People to Your Poster*

Senior folks: Bring other senior people you know to your students' poster!

Nothing is more validating than presenting your work to researchers whom you look up to.

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1518097924290490368-1.jpg)](media/1518097924290490368-1.mp4)

*Make a Good Poster*

To make a good poster presentation, you need to make a good poster!

All the presentation tips won't work if you have a crappy poster.

## How to revise an academic poster?

原帖：<https://x.com/jbhuang0604/status/1974198882906325054> · 2025-10-03 · 共 18 条串文

How to Revise an Academic Poster?

Designing a clear poster is an essential skill for students. We recently revised one for a paper presented last week.

BUT, instead of just one student benefiting from the process, I’m sharing it and hope some will find it helpful. 🧵

In the initial draft, it's common to simply include most of the figures and tables from the paper on the poster.

This is a good start.

BUT, this often results in too much detail, making the poster less effective at communicating the main message.

![image](media/1974198884621824044-1.jpg)

In the 2nd draft, we highlight the key messages (problem, key idea, results).

BUT, there are many design/layout/writing choices that we can improve.

Let's take a closer look at them! Shall we?

![image](media/1974198888035946975-1.jpg)

Many posters have these types of school logos.

BUT, nobody really knows them, and they are simply too small to be seen. 🔍

A better way is to simply spell out the affiliations instead.

![image](media/1974198891412328647-1.png)

The author list is important.

As a junior student, you want to make this part clear and obvious so that other people remember you.

Details:
- make it large
- replace commas with spaces
- fix the superscript
- use line breaks so that the author list is center-aligned.

![image](media/1974198894017065439-1.jpg)

Here, @JayLEE_0301 started with a question.

BUT, there are two problems with this question.
- 1) unclear what "structured" or "human-like" means
- 2) the sentence has TWO lines

Always write one-liners! Ain't nobody got time to read!

![image](media/1974198896546169204-1.jpg)

Bullet points

Whenever we use bullet points, we should use parallel writing (or parallel structure).

This helps reduce the cognitive load on your readers when parsing the text.

(not a fan of many colors, but it's okay as they help establish correspondence with other parts)

![image](media/1974198899159281891-1.jpg)

Building up correspondence!

BEFORE:
- unclear relation between title and figure
- changing icons

AFTER:
- tie the three figures with the title
- enlarge the text annotation.

![image](media/1974198901680058640-1.jpg)

Let's improve the illustration slightly.

Here, we aim to convey the concept of possible outcomes (e.g., an unsupported block will slide down).

BUT, we only show the final state.

Adding "history" and "trajectory" makes the illusion clearer.

![image](media/1974198904326660196-1.png)

Write concisely and avoid multi-line sentences at all costs!

![image](media/1974198906599977364-1.jpg)

Now to the middle column.

Here, the sentence has parallel structure. That's great!

BUT, the layout does not reinforce the parallel concept.

Let's align them so we can clearly see the parallel structure.

![image](media/1974216610417176844-1.png)

The method overview figure is tricky.

It needs to have a proper level of abstraction, yet a sufficient level of detail to illustrate how things work.

Here, we adjust the layout and add a few annotations to improve its clarity.

![image](media/1974221204052488632-1.jpg)

Unless it's a quote,  avoid writing complete sentences.

Those sentences you intended to write are something you will SAY in a presentation.

Let people pay attention to you, not just the poster/slides.

![image](media/1974222188451381694-1.jpg)

For results, the initial draft is yet another list.

I think it would be fun to present it visually for a change.

Unfortunately, I was told that robotics people are quite conservative and they will not like this.

So my revision suggestion was rejected...

![image](media/1974226235921228141-1.jpg)

BEFORE

Two issues with the original result presentation
- unclear correspondence because the texts are not on the same line
- the blue color is a bit arbitrary

AFTER
- fix the alignment
- user green to invoke the concept of *gain* (from stock markets)

![image](media/1974228128579891348-1.jpg)

When presenting results in tables, be sure to include annotations (e.g., success rate) to provide context.

Without such information, it can be difficult for readers to interpret the results accurately.

![image](media/1974228980338237610-1.jpg)

Let's put all these together. Here is the revised poster.

There are still many areas for improvement, such as the overview figure, excessive text, excessive colors, and layout design.

But, with these changes, it's now a decent poster presentation!

![image](media/1974233905315119219-1.jpg)
