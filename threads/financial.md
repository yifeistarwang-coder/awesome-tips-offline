## How to save for retirement as a graduate student (in the US)?

How to save for retirement as a graduate student (in the US)?

*Why?*

"Don't make fun of graduate students. They just made a terrible life choice." - The Simpsons

Yes, grad students are paid poorly. But with income from RA/TA/fellowships/internships, there usually are leftover money.

❌ Do nothing
✅ Invest in *tax-advantaged* accounts

*Why save early?*

When you save and invest for retirement early, you get to enjoy the power of compounding for your investments.

Ideally, max out the contribution limit (6K a year).

*How to save?*

Two types of Individual Retirement Accounts (IRAs):

👉 Traditional: Save now, tax later
👉 Roth: Tax now, save later

As grad students are in the low-tax bracket, Roth IRA would probably make more sense.

Earnings growth + withdrawals in retirement: *tax free*

*Why now?*

Previously, graduate students who are on fellowship stipends or grants would have difficulty in funding their IRAs because those income are not considered "taxable compensation".

But that change in Dec 2019, making it easy for all grad students to save!

[![GIF 动画预览](media/1510479721179586560-1.jpg)](media/1510479721179586560-1.mp4)

*What if I really need the money after graduation?*

✅ Withdraw Roth IRA contributions (not earnings) are tax free and penalty free.

✅ Several early withdrawals of earnings can still be tax/penalty free, e.g., 1st home purchase, large medical expenses, education.

Disclaimer: Clearly this is NOT a financial advice. You should definitely check out more information and make your own decisions.

But, I wish I knew about all these options and tax-benefits when I was a grad student (especially for international students).
